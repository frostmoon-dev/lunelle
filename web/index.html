    How this works

    The projection uses the published dynamic body-weight model from Hall et al. (The Lancet, 2011), the model behind the NIH Body Weight Planner. It accounts for the fact that you burn less as you get lighter, so weight loss slows down and eventually levels off at a plateau.

    The first one or two weeks drop faster: that is mostly stored carbohydrate (glycogen) and the water held with it, not fat.

    The dashed line shows the old "3,500 calories = 1 pound" rule for comparison. It assumes your deficit never shrinks, so it overestimates loss over time.

    The shaded band shows what happens if your real energy needs are 5% higher or lower than the estimate. Logging your actual weight over a few weeks is the best way to find out where you are in that band.
